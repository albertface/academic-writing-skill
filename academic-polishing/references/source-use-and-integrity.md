# Source Use And Integrity

Use this reference for polishing text that includes quotations, paraphrases, summaries, citations, literature review, or source-based argument.

## Core Rule

Preserve academic integrity. Improve expression and integration, but do not invent sources, page numbers, author names, dates, quotations, findings, or citation details.

## Quotation

When polishing quoted material:

- Do not change words inside quotation marks unless the user asks and the change is marked appropriately.
- Preserve quotation marks, ellipses, brackets, and citation markers.
- Improve the signal phrase or surrounding explanation if the quotation is dropped into the paragraph without context.
- Ensure the sentence after the quotation explains its relevance when needed.

## Paraphrase

A good paraphrase restates source information in new wording and structure while preserving meaning and citation.

When revising paraphrases:

- Change both sentence structure and wording; synonym swaps alone are weak.
- Keep technical terms unchanged when they are necessary.
- Keep the source citation attached to the paraphrased material.
- Avoid making the source claim broader, narrower, more certain, or more current than stated.

## Summary

A summary condenses the main idea and major supporting points.

When polishing summaries:

- Remove minor examples unless they are essential to the user's point.
- Keep the summary neutral unless the user is explicitly evaluating the source.
- Make the relationship between summary and argument clear.
- Preserve source attribution.

## Citation-Aware Editing

When text contains citations:

- Keep citation tokens exactly as supplied unless fixing obvious spacing or punctuation.
- Do not harmonize citation style beyond the visible local pattern unless asked.
- If a claim appears uncited and likely needs support, flag it rather than adding a fake citation.
- If citation placement is ambiguous, place the citation at the end of the relevant borrowed idea, not after unrelated commentary.

## Literature Review Flow

Polish literature review paragraphs so they do more than list sources.

Use this progression when possible:

1. Topic sentence naming the theme, debate, method, or gap.
2. Grouped source evidence, not one isolated sentence per paper when synthesis is possible.
3. Contrast, convergence, limitation, or unresolved question.
4. Link back to the study's purpose or argument.

## Plagiarism Risk Flags

Flag text when:

- source wording appears too close to an original passage supplied by the user;
- paraphrased material lacks citation;
- quotation marks are missing around distinctive borrowed wording;
- patchwriting is visible through minor synonym replacement;
- the author claims another source's idea as their own.

Offer a revised paraphrase or a note such as: "This sentence appears to need a citation or quotation marks, depending on whether it is paraphrased or quoted."
